# Storage evidence

Storage datasets in this directory are literature or project-data references. They are not runtime defaults unless an individual dataset is explicitly mapped through the parameter system and passes its activation gates.

`roadside_grass_silage_duration_codigestion_homeky_2016_abstract_reference.csv` adds the three durations and relative methane-yield changes available in Homeky et al.'s publisher abstract: roadside grass ensiled without additives for 3, 8, or 36 weeks and co-digested with cow slurry. The reported changes are versus slurry-only controls, with two batch tests per mixture and no additional inoculum. The abstract does not give absolute yields, the grass/slurry ratio, storage conditions, or variability. These values do not isolate storage loss or the grass-only methane potential and must not be used to calibrate GreenHub storage or digestion. The records are B-tier abstract-level primary evidence and reference-only.

Source: Homeky et al. (2016), DOI [10.1002/ep.12361](https://doi.org/10.1002/ep.12361); [publisher abstract](https://aiche.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ep.12361). Full text was not accessible in this collection pass.

`post_opening_aerobic_silage_emissions_krommweh_2020_reference.csv` adds eight cumulative measurements reported in the abstract of Krommweh et al. (2020), covering CO2, methanol, ethanol and ethyl acetate from grass and lucerne silage bales during the first 600 hours after opening. Values are per bale. Bale mass, per-tonne factors and per-species uncertainty are not reported in the abstract. The dataset therefore contributes contextual evidence for post-opening aerobic exposure but does not parameterize sealed storage, storage kinetics, methane or nitrous-oxide emissions, or LCA impact factors. It remains B-tier and reference-only.

Source: Krommweh et al. (2020), DOI [10.1016/j.envpol.2020.115513](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envpol.2020.115513); bibliographic record and abstract at [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32891044/).

`grass_silage_fresh_matter_loss_stability_hindrichsen_2025_reference.csv` transcribes Table 1 and one explicitly conditioned result from the 2025 International Silage Conference abstract compilation. It distinguishes reported fresh-matter losses during anaerobic storage and aerobic exposure from aerobic-stability time; these are not dry-matter loss coefficients. The underlying synthesis covers cultivated grass silage from Denmark, Lithuania and Germany, has no reported pooled n or trial-level rows, and was authored by staff of the inoculant supplier. It is B-tier literature and reference-only, not a GreenHub or roadside-grass storage model input. See its `.source.json` sidecar and `source_register.csv` entry for basis, limitations and reuse-rights status.

Source: Hindrichsen, Camargo do Amaral and Witt (2025), International Silage Conference abstract compilation, [public PDF](https://conference.ifas.ufl.edu/silage/documents/Silage-2025-Abstract-Compilation.pdf); no DOI or explicit reuse license identified.

`silage_feedout_co2_ethanol_emissions_shan_2026_abstract_reference.csv` preserves four area-normalized CO2/ethanol emission summaries for the first 0–10 hours after exposing maize and ryegrass mini-silos to air, plus two approximate CO2/ethanol emission lags. The publisher abstract does not identify the statistic represented by the ± term, specify its denominator beyond area, or separate results by additive treatment; underlying data are available on request. These crop-silage jar measurements are not GreenHub roadside-grass emission factors or storage kinetics. The records remain B-tier, reference-only, and are not connected to runtime or LCA calculations.

Source: Shan et al. (2026), DOI [10.1016/j.jece.2026.121987](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jece.2026.121987); [publisher article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213343726009619).

`high_moisture_grass_silage_fermentation_li_nishino_2013_reference.csv` transcribes pre-ensiling DM/pH and the grass-only physicochemical rows from Tables 2–3 of Li and Nishino (2013). It follows Italian ryegrass and guinea-grass silages across 3–120 days of sealed storage and includes the source's seven-day aerobic challenge at 25 °C for 14-, 28-, 56- and 120-day silages. Table-level SEs and significance markers are retained. Values are B-tier measured-literature evidence, but they are cultivated forage silage, not Dutch roadside/GreenHub feed; ambient sealed-storage temperature is not quantified, and chemistry concentrations are not mass-loss or emission measurements. The source reports conflicting Italian ryegrass WSC values in Table 1 and the Results text, so WSC is intentionally omitted. No explicit open reuse license was identified. The data remain reference-only and do not parameterize the storage model.

Source: Li and Nishino (2013), *Asian-Australasian Journal of Animal Sciences* 26(9), 1304–1312, DOI [10.5713/ajas.2013.13175](https://doi.org/10.5713/ajas.2013.13175); [full text at PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4093406/).

`wet_green_protein_paste_storage_santamaria_stodkilde_2019_2021_reference.csv` adds a compact Phase 2/4 comparator for storage of extracted wet protein paste. It contains only explicit prose values and study-design facts: initial wet-paste DM/OM for alfalfa, red clover and ryegrass; two reported 12-month pH endpoints; the clover-grass abstract's initial protein/amino-acid values; tested storage design; and author-reported maximum stable durations at 4 °C and 24 °C. Plotted points are not digitized, and no storage curve or stability threshold is inferred. These are product-paste observations—not raw-grass storage losses, roadside feed, or GreenHub product specifications. Neither source states an explicit data-reuse license; source documents are not copied, and rights review is required before external redistribution. The 2017 report's bag-count statement is internally inconsistent, so no replicate count is used as a model input.

Sources: Santamaria Fernandez and Stødkilde, *Shelf life of the wet protein pastes upon storage*, [Organic Eprints record 36872](https://orgprints.org/36872/1/D1.4%20Research%20report_Shelf%20life%20of%20wet%20protein%20pastes.pdf); Steenfeldt, Engberg and Ambye-Jensen, OK-Net EcoFeed practice abstract, [Organic Eprints record 39520](https://orgprints.org/id/eprint/39520/1/PA-AU-green-protein-testing-layers-final.pdf).

`jinmu_grain_grass_silage_storage_time_tang_2025_reference.csv` transcribes Tables 1–2 from Tang et al. (2025) for 0, 3, 7, 15, 30 and 60 days of ensiling. It preserves means, SEM, time-effect p-values, significance letters and non-detects for fermentation indicators, plus the Table 1 nutritional composition values. Table 1 omits units and bases for five composition metrics; these are explicitly marked unknown, not filled from convention. Five bags were sampled at each time, and storage temperature is not reported. This is a cultivated Jinmu/Pennisetum grass comparator from Xinjiang, not roadside grass, and it does not provide mass loss or gas emissions. CC BY 4.0; B-tier, reference-only, no runtime mapping. Source: Tang et al. (2025), DOI [10.3390/microorganisms13091973](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms13091973); [open article and tables](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12471645/).

`cultivated_ryegrass_festulolium_ensiling_proteolysis_muhandiram_2023_reference.csv` transcribes Tables 1–3 from Muhandiram et al. (2023): fresh-forage DM/WSC/total N and Cut 2/Cut 4 silage DM, pH, WSC, total N, soluble N and ammonia-N over seven points from 0 h to 90 d. It retains cut/cultivar identity, means across time, marginal time means, factorial SEM/p-values and significance annotations. The ensiling timepoints are stored as reported labels; elapsed hours are additionally encoded using 1 d = 24 h. The study used cultivated UK ryegrass and Festulolium, wilted 24 h, inoculated, and stored in laboratory tube silos at 21 °C. CC BY 4.0; B-tier, reference-only. Concentrations are not retained mass, effluent, emissions or roadside-grass kinetics. Source: Muhandiram et al. (2023), DOI [10.1002/fes3.475](https://doi.org/10.1002/fes3.475); [open article and tables](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10909542/).

`paiaguas_grass_silage_storage_loss_effluent_saute_2021_reference.csv` transcribes 114 records from Saute et al. (2021), Tables 2–4 and specified Methods/Results passages. The records retain 90-day storage outcomes as factor marginal means, five initial-forage values and one bounded aerobic-exposure observation. This is cultivated tropical Paiaguás grass in laboratory silos, not roadside grass. Factor interactions are not reconstructed; ambiguous units and bases remain flagged. The dataset is CC BY 4.0, B-tier, and reference-only; it is not mapped to storage kinetics or runtime defaults. See its `.source.json` sidecar for full FAIR metadata, provenance, checksum and source ambiguities.

Source: Saute et al. (2021), DOI [10.1590/1678-4162-12197](https://doi.org/10.1590/1678-4162-12197); [open article](https://www.scielo.br/j/abmvz/a/dBKkfPD6rTsFHQx6yzP95jG/?lang=en).

`grass_silage_storage_system_losses_randby_bakken_2021_reference.csv` transcribes the abstract and Table 3 outcomes from Randby and Bakken (2021), including initial crop-DM lows, bunker/bale density comparisons, and offered/wasted/invisible mass disposition for bunker, round-bale and laboratory-silo treatments. Table 3 preserves both fresh-crop and initial-crop-DM denominators and group SEMs. Invisible losses are not split into respiration, effluent, fermentation, aerobic deterioration or gaseous pathways; round-bale losses include an author-estimated effluent correction. The source is cultivated Norwegian grass-clover, not roadside/VARTA grass; common numeric storage days are not reported, so no kinetics or GreenHub default are inferred. The open article is CC BY 4.0 and the extract remains B-tier, reference-only.

Source: Randby and Bakken (2021), DOI [10.1016/j.anifeedsci.2021.114868](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anifeedsci.2021.114868); [publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377840121000547); [author-posted full text and license](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349116084_Bunkers_or_round_bales_Losses_and_silage_quality_with_or_without_acid_treatment_of_low_dry_matter_grass_crops).

`wet_lowland_grass_silage_storage_losses_kristensen_feng_moller_2016_reference.csv` transcribes Tables 1–3 from a Danish wet-lowland grass pilot study. It records source-specific wet-mass and dry-matter-loss percentages for tall fescue, Festulolium and reed canary grass after 144, 223 and 304 days, with no-additive, Sill-All Fireguard and Biotal Axcool Gold treatments. Species values and the paper's cross-species averages are distinct record scopes. Triplicate 60 L airtight barrels are described, but the tables give no dispersion statistics or percentage denominator. Negative mass losses are preserved exactly as printed; the paper attributes small mass differences to weighing accuracy and notes moisture changes. These cultivated grasses are not roadside/VARTA feed, and the data do not establish a transferable GreenHub kinetic equation. The paper is publicly linked from Aarhus University's research portal, but no explicit reuse license was identified. The dataset remains B-tier, D3/reference-only.

Source: Kristensen, Feng and Møller (2016), International Conference on Agricultural Engineering, [Aarhus University research record](https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/storage-and-pretreatment-of-grass-for-from-extensive-lowland-area); [public conference paper](https://conferences.au.dk/uploads/tx_powermail/energy_production_of_biomass__cigr_ageng_full_paper.pdf).
